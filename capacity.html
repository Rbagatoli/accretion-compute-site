The model

Everything around the rack

Accretion is a capacity provider, not a cloud. We do not sell GPU-hours or compete with our tenants. We build and operate the site; you own and run your workloads.

  • Power delivered to your racks from on-site generation
  • Cooling designed for your density, air or liquid
  • Space in secure halls with controlled physical access
  • Connectivity through fiber routes and cross-connects
  • Operations: on-site staff and remote hands

Anatomy of a site

One site, from fuel to fiber

Energy enters on one side. Compute leaves on the other. Step through each part of the site to see what it does and who runs it.

Fuel arrives at the fence line

Accretion runs it

Gas reaches the site from a pipeline, a producer's gathering system or a landfill's collection system. It is metered at the boundary and cleaned up before it reaches the engines.

  • Supply agreements that run for the length of your lease
  • Metering, filtering and conditioning on site
  • A second supply path wherever the site allows it

Generation on site, behind the meter

Accretion runs it

Engines or turbines turn the gas into electricity a short walk from your racks. The plant is sized to your load, with more units than the load needs so any one can be serviced while the rest carry it.

  • No wait in a utility interconnection queue
  • Spare units designed in for maintenance
  • Room to add units as your deployment grows

Power, conditioned and protected

Accretion runs it

Switchgear, transformers and uninterruptible power supplies turn raw generation into clean, steady power, then carry it to the hall on feeds designed for your rack layout.

  • Switchgear and transformers sized to the site
  • UPS to ride through any switchover
  • Feeds to the rack, matched to your power design

A hall built around your hardware

You run what is inside your racks

The hall is designed to your density, layout and cooling method, from the floor loading to the containment. You install and run your hardware; we run the building around it.

  • Layout and density set by your deployment
  • Secure, controlled physical access
  • On-site staff and remote hands

Heat out, at the density AI makes

Accretion runs it

Heat leaves the hall through air handling, liquid loops or both, and is rejected outside. The cooling plant is designed for the heat your racks will actually make.

  • Air, rear-door or direct-to-chip liquid cooling
  • Liquid loops to the rack for dense accelerators
  • Designed to your hardware's heat split

Fiber to the network and your users

Accretion runs the routes

Sites are chosen with fiber in reach. Routes come into the site, and cross-connects carry them to your racks, so data moves as freely as the power does.

  • Fiber routes to the site
  • Cross-connects to your racks
  • You choose your carriers and network design

Density

Built for the heat AI makes

An AI rack can draw many times the power of a conventional one, and nearly all of that power ends up as heat. Halls designed for yesterday's racks cannot simply be filled with today's. We design power and cooling around the hardware you will actually run.

Conventional server rack3 to 5 kW
Present-day GPU rackUp to about 120 kW

Figures from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and a 2025 Pacific Northwest National Laboratory report for the US Department of Energy. More in our guide to liquid cooling.

Air

Cold air through the front of the rack, hot air out the back, kept apart by containment. Simple and familiar.

Suits lower-density racks and the parts of an AI rack liquid does not reach

Rear-door

A water-cooled coil on the back of the rack takes the heat out of the exhaust air before it reaches the room.

A middle path for denser racks that are still air-cooled

Direct-to-chip

Liquid runs through cold plates on the processors and carries most of the heat away. Air handles what is left.

Built for the densest accelerators

How it works

From first call to running workloads

  1. Requirements

    Power, density, cooling, location and timeline. We start with what your deployment needs.

    • You share your hardware plan and timeline
    • We map it to power and cooling needs

  2. Site match

    We match your requirements to sites in our pipeline where energy, land and fiber line up.

    • We propose a site with a dated plan
    • You review the site and the terms

  3. Build to spec

    We design and build the hall, power and cooling to your specification.

    • We build and commission the site
    • You sign off the design and install your hardware

  4. Operate

    Our team runs the site day to day. You run your workloads.

    • We run power, cooling and the building
    • You run your hardware and workloads

Questions

Capacity FAQ

Is capacity available today?

Not yet. Accretion Compute is in development, and we are matching early tenants with sites now. If you are planning capacity, talk to us about your timeline.

Do you own or rent out GPUs?

No. Tenants bring and own their hardware. We provide the energy, building, power, cooling, connectivity and on-site operations around it.

Where will your sites be?

We are evaluating sites in Canada and the United States where firm energy, fiber and land come together. See how we choose sites.

How much power can a site provide?

Each site is sized to its energy supply and to the tenant's needs. We share figures for a specific site once it is matched to your requirements, not before.

How long until power is ready?

It depends on the site and the build. Each proposal includes a dated plan. Generating on site means we do not wait in a utility interconnection queue, which is often the longest step for a grid-connected build. See how long a data center takes to build.

What uptime and redundancy will you offer?

Each site's design, redundancy and service levels are set out in its contract before you commit. We do not publish figures for sites that are not yet built.

Can you support liquid cooling?

Yes. Halls are designed to the tenant's specification, including direct-to-chip liquid cooling for high-density accelerators.

Can we expand later?

Yes. We look for sites with room to grow, in both energy and land, and expansion rights can be written into the lease.
