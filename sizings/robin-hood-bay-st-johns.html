Landfill gas · sized from the City's figures

Robin Hood Bay Regional Waste Management Facility, St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador

The gas the City's flare burns today can run an AI data center of about 2.4 MW.

What the City gets

  • The City keeps every environmental credit it holds today; new credits the project creates are shared on terms we agree.
  • Revenue on gas the flare now burns for nothing.
  • A land lease for about 8 acres near the flare.
  • No capital from the City for the engines or the data center. The City's collection system stays as it is; the only compliance changes are the permit updates mapped below.

How the gas is paid for: about $394,000 to $596,000 a year ($3.9 million to $6.0 million over a 10-year term): a land lease of $1,000 to $2,000 an acre a year, plus the gas at $2.00 to $3.00 per MMBtu. The gas price is a floor price plus a share of what the power from the gas is worth, higher for gas that meets the engines' quality spec, set once we've looked at the site together.

Permits we've mapped

  • Power on site: own use, as we read it: one company on one site, selling power to no one, under the Electrical Power Control Act, 1994 and the Public Utilities Act.
  • Federal methane rules: the Landfill Methane Regulations (SOR/2025-279) set duties from January 1, 2028 for sites with a gas system. Our engines count as a destruction device (equipment that burns the methane), and the flare stays as backup.
  • Provincial approvals: a certificate of approval from the Province and, if the Province asks for it, Environmental Assessment registration. Worth confirming

As of November 18, 2026. Lines marked “Worth confirming” are our reading, to check with the agency.

What it would look like

  • Our own gas engines, a battery to smooth the load, and factory-built compute modules on a fenced parcel near the flare.
  • Engines and computers share one 480-volt system on site: no substation, no grid tie.
  • We build and run the site and lease the compute modules to an AI company.
