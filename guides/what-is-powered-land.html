What Is Powered Land? Meaning, Value and How to Check a Site

For landowners and energy owners: what "powered" really means, how powered land differs from a powered shell, and the evidence that makes a power claim real.

Updated November 14, 2026 · By Accretion Compute team · 7 min read

Quick answer

Powered land is a site with a secured path to large amounts of electricity: a utility commitment to serve a large load, or firm energy that can be generated on or next to the site. The term has no legal definition. What counts is the evidence behind the power, plus fiber, zoning, water and buildable acres.

The phrase turns up in land listings, broker decks and news about record sales, usually without a definition. That is a problem for an owner, because "powered" can mean anything from a signed utility agreement to a transmission line you can see from the road.

This guide is for landowners and energy owners. It covers what powered land means, how it differs from a powered shell, the two routes to it, and the evidence that separates a real power path from a hopeful one. If you are weighing a sale against a lease, start with our guide to selling or leasing land to a data center.

What does powered land mean?

Powered land is land where the hard part of a data center project, a large and steady supply of electricity, is already solved or close to it. The powered land meaning most buyers use has two parts: a number of megawatts, and a date by which they can be delivered.

There is no legal or industry-standard definition, and each seller draws the line in a different place. The documents behind the label matter more than the label.

Claims of "powered" come in three strengths:

  • Near power. A transmission line or substation is close by. This is geography, not capacity.
  • In the queue. Someone has applied to the utility or grid operator for a large load connection. This is a place in line.
  • Committed. A signed agreement states how many megawatts arrive and when, or firm energy is under contract to generate power at the site.

Only the third is powered land in the sense a buyer will pay for. A powered land data center site usually has fiber, zoning, a cooling plan and enough flat ground in hand or in reach as well. Power takes longest to get, so it gives the land its name and most of its value.

Powered land vs a powered shell: what is the difference?

Powered land is ground with a power path and no building. A powered shell is one step further: a finished, weather-tight building with utility power and fiber brought to it, handed over before the tenant fits out the inside.

People who search for "what is land power shell" are usually looking at these two terms side by side, because listings and lease offers use both. They sit on one ladder, from bare ground to a running facility.

In a powered shell the tenant usually installs backup power, cooling and racks. In a turnkey data center the landlord delivers those too, and the tenant brings only its computing hardware. The split changes from deal to deal, so read the scope in the contract and not the label.

Why do buyers pay for the power and not the acres?

Because power is the slowest and least certain part of a data center project. Land that already has it lets a buyer start years sooner. Acres are easy to find.

The U.S. Department of Energy says large electricity users need connection "on timelines shorter than current development allows," and lists permitting delays and interconnection queues among the barriers.

In Texas, the grid operator told state senators in July 2026 that it was tracking over 470 GW of large loads seeking a connection, about 90% of them data centers. Over 200 GW of that had the studies needed to enter its first batch review. A request is not a connection.

Equipment adds to the wait. A 2024 Department of Energy report to Congress found lead times of about three years commonly quoted for large power transformers, and up to 60 months at the longest.

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory estimates that U.S. data centers will need over 140 GW of grid interconnection capacity by 2030 in its reference case. Our guide to the data center interconnection queue explains why the waits run long.

What are the two routes to powered land?

Land gets powered in one of two ways: the grid commits to serve it, or the energy is on the site or next door. Many owners think only of the first.

  • Grid-powered land. A utility has agreed to serve a large load at the site, usually after a load study, with any line or substation upgrades scheduled. Spare capacity on a map is not a commitment. Our guide on what it costs to bring power to land covers who pays for those upgrades.
  • Energy-powered land. The power is generated on the site or taken straight from a plant next door, behind the meter, without waiting for a grid connection. The energy can be natural gas, landfill gas, hydro, nuclear, geothermal, or wind and solar with storage. It has to be firm: steady around the clock and contractable for years.

Government guidance treats the second route as normal. The Environmental Protection Agency's January 2026 guidance on siting AI data centers notes that they are often built directly next to or together with substations or power plants, and that on-site generation is often needed. The Berkeley Lab report adds that some data centers may skip grid interconnection altogether and build their own generation.

In April 2025 the Department of Energy listed 16 federal sites as suited to rapid data center construction, citing in-place energy infrastructure, and in July 2025 it selected four of them.

If your land holds a retired plant, see brownfield data centers. If it holds a landfill, see landfill gas to energy.

How do you prove that land is powered?

With documents. A powered land claim is as strong as the paper behind it, and a serious buyer asks for that paper at the start.

For grid-powered land:

  • the utility's written response to a large load request, with the megawatts and the in-service date
  • the load study and the upgrades it calls for, and who pays for them
  • the service or interconnection agreement, if one is signed
  • whether the capacity is tied to the land or to the applicant

For energy-powered land:

  • what the energy source is, how much it delivers and how steady it has been, from metered records
  • who owns it, and whether a long-term supply agreement is possible
  • what backs it up when the main source is down
  • the permit path for generating power on site

On either route the rest of the site still has to work: fiber within reach, zoning that allows a data center, a cooling plan, road access and enough buildable ground. EPA's guidance says a 100 MW campus typically needs at least 100 acres, with fewer megawatts fitting on less. Our data center site selection guide walks through each test.

Who does powered land development, and what do powered land companies do?

Powered land development is the work of turning a parcel into a site with a committed power path, then selling or leasing it to whoever builds. Several kinds of firms do it, often grouped together as powered land companies.

  • Land developers. They take an option on a parcel, file the load request, post the deposits and win the zoning, then sell or lease the site.
  • Energy developers and plant owners. They start from a power plant, a gas supply or a wind and solar project, and bring land and a data center user to it. That makes them data center power companies.
  • Energy-first operators. They build the generation and the data center together on the owner's site and lease the finished capacity. Accretion Compute works this way.

Whoever approaches you, ask the same things. Who files the power request, and in whose name? Who pays for studies and deposits? What do you receive during the option period, and how long can it run? If the power never arrives, does the land come back to you free of claims?

This guide is general information, not legal, tax or investment advice. Have a real estate lawyer read any option, lease or purchase agreement before you sign.

Powered land for sale: how should you read a listing?

Read a powered land for sale listing for three things: megawatts, a date and a document. If one is missing, treat the site as raw land with a good location.

A credible listing states how many megawatts are committed, by whom and by when, and names the distance to the substation or energy source. Phrases like "power nearby" or "utility in discussions" describe a hope.

There is no reliable public figure for what powered land sells for per acre or per megawatt. Reported prices come from sites that differ in megawatts, timing and location, and most terms are private.

How can you tell if your land could become powered land?

Start with energy, since it decides the rest.

  1. List the energy on or near the land. A gas pipeline or wells, a landfill, a hydro, nuclear or geothermal plant, wind or solar with storage, a substation.
  2. Ask whether it is firm. Steady supply that can be contracted for years counts. Gas that is flared, interruptible or declining suits loads that can switch off, such as Bitcoin mining.
  3. Find the fiber. Long-haul routes often follow highways, railways and pipelines.
  4. Measure the buildable ground. Take out floodplain, wetlands, steep slopes and easements.
  5. Check zoning. Industrial zoning, or a realistic path to it, saves a long fight.
  6. Decide what you want. A sale, a long lease, or a partnership that also sells your energy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between powered land and a powered shell?

Powered land is a site with a secured power path and no building. A powered shell is a finished building with utility power and fiber delivered to it, before the tenant installs backup power, cooling and racks. Both stop short of a turnkey data center, which is ready for the tenant's servers.

How much is powered land worth per acre?

There is no reliable public figure. Value follows the megawatts a site can deliver and how soon, not its acreage, so two parcels of the same size can be far apart in price. Most sale terms stay private. A review of your own site's power evidence is the only sound basis for a number.

Does powered land need a grid connection?

No. A site can be powered by energy generated on it or taken directly from a plant next door, behind the meter. Natural gas, landfill gas, hydro, nuclear, geothermal, and wind and solar with storage can all serve, provided the supply is firm and under contract.

What do powered land companies do?

They turn a parcel into a site with a committed power path, then sell or lease it. The work includes optioning the land, filing a large load request or lining up an energy supply, paying for studies and deposits, and securing zoning. Check who carries the cost and how long your land is tied up.

How long does it take to turn raw land into powered land?

There is no standard figure. On the grid route the wait is set by the utility's studies, upgrades and equipment; a 2024 Department of Energy report found large power transformers commonly quoted at about three years. On the energy route it depends on the supply contract, the permits and building the generation.
