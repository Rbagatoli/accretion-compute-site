Firm power first

AI cannot pause. Its power cannot either.

AI tenants need power around the clock for the full length of a lease. So we only build on energy that is firm: steady in volume, contracted, and backed up.

Interruptible or declining sources, like flare gas, are better suited to Bitcoin mining, which our sister company Proton Mining runs. Accretion focuses on supply that lasts.

From molecule to megawatt

Every step between the gas and your racks

On a behind-the-meter site, the whole path from fuel to rack sits inside one fence, run by one team.

  1. Gas in

    Fuel arrives by pipeline or collection system and is metered at the fence.

  2. Conditioning

    Filtering and treatment bring the gas to the quality the engines need.

  3. Generators

    Engines or turbines turn the gas into electricity, with spare units on hand.

  4. Switchgear

    Protects the system and routes power between units and loads.

  5. Transformers

    Step the voltage to what the hall's equipment uses.

  6. UPS

    Batteries carry the load through any switchover, without a flicker.

  7. Distribution

    Feeds carry power across the hall to each row.

  8. Your racks

    Power arrives at your racks, matched to your power design.

Sources

Energy we build on

Four kinds of site, each with its own strengths. What they share is supply that is steady, contracted and long-lived.

Pipeline gas

Sites near transmission or distribution pipelines, with a tap and meter station feeding on-site generation. Pipelines carry gas from many producers, so supply does not hang on any one well.

  • Pipeline capacity available at the site
  • Long-term supply and transport agreements
  • Fiber and land within reach

Gathering-system gas

Gas from a producer's gathering network, taken near the wells before it reaches a processing plant. We look for systems with steady volumes and build a second supply path into the design.

  • Steady, documented production volumes
  • A backup supply path from the start
  • A producer looking for a long-term buyer

Landfill gas

Decomposing waste gives off methane for decades. Collection wells capture it and feed engines on site, turning a gas that would otherwise be flared or vented into power.

  • An established collection system
  • Gas flow that can be measured and forecast
  • A landfill operator looking for steady use of its gas

Industrial sites

Existing or former industrial sites where power infrastructure, permits and land are already in place. Reusing them can save years compared with starting from an empty field.

  • Electrical infrastructure already on site
  • Land zoned for industrial use
  • Room to add generation and halls

When one unit comes out for service, the others carry the full load, and your racks never notice. The same spare capacity covers an unexpected fault.

Behind the meter

A shorter path from energy to compute

A grid-connected data centre waits for power to be generated far away and carried in. Behind the meter, generation sits on the same site as the load.

No queue to wait in

Grid connections for large loads can take years to approve and build. On-site generation does not wait for one.

Fewer steps to fail

No long-distance lines between the plant and the load. Generation, power and the hall are run by one team on one site.

Costs you can see

Energy is billed from the site's own supply, separately and transparently, rather than through a utility tariff.

Questions

Energy FAQ

What does behind the meter mean?

The power is generated on the same site where it is used, instead of being bought from a utility over the grid. See behind-the-meter data centers.

Why gas?

Gas can be delivered steadily, around the clock, and stored and backed up on site, which suits loads that run continuously. Generating from it on site avoids waiting for a grid connection. See gas-powered data centers.

Why not flare gas?

Flare gas often comes from wells whose output falls over time or stops without warning. AI tenants need power for years, so we build on supply that is steady and contracted for the length of the lease.

What happens if a generator fails?

Sites are designed with more generating units than the load needs, plus UPS batteries to ride through any switchover. The exact redundancy design for each site is set out in its contract.

Will sites connect to the grid?

Some may, for backup or to export spare power, where it makes sense for the site. The core design does not depend on it.

How is energy billed?

Capacity is priced per kW per month. Energy is billed separately and transparently, from the site's own supply.
