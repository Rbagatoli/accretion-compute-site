The overlap

Energy, fiber and land, in one place

Plenty of places have gas. Plenty have fiber. Plenty have open land. Far fewer have all three, steady enough and close enough to build on for years.

That overlap is what we search for. Most grid-connected projects start from where the grid has spare capacity. We start from where the energy is, and generate on site.

What we look for

Six things every site needs

Pick a criterion to see what we check, and what would rule a site out.

Firm fuel supply

Enough gas, steady enough, for the full term of a lease, with a backup supply path. Fuel carries the most weight in our scoring: without it, nothing else matters.

  • Volumes, history and forecasts for the source
  • Gas quality and what it needs before the engines
  • A second supply path, such as pipeline backup
  • Supply terms that can match a multi-year lease

Rules a site out: supply that is short-lived or can be cut off.

Fiber in reach

Existing fiber routes close enough to connect the site without years of construction, and ideally more than one, so the site is not hanging on a single line.

  • Distance to existing long-haul and regional routes
  • More than one route, from more than one carrier
  • Time and permits to build the last stretch

Rules a site out: no realistic way to connect it in time.

Near people

A reasonable distance to population centres: close enough for the workloads that need low latency, and for the people who build and run the site to live nearby.

  • Network latency to the tenant's users, where it matters
  • A local workforce for building and operations
  • Services, suppliers and housing within reach

Matters less for training, which can run far from users.

Room to expand

Land and fuel to grow with the tenant, so the second phase does not need a second site. AI deployments tend to grow, and moving is expensive.

  • Buildable land beyond the first phase
  • Gas that can support more generation later
  • Expansion rights in the land and supply agreements

Rules a site out: no path past the first phase.

Clear regulatory path

Permitting, environmental and local rules we can work through on a predictable timeline. A site that cannot be permitted in time is not a site.

  • Zoning and land use
  • Environmental and air permits for on-site generation
  • Local support, and a timeline we can plan around

Rules a site out: permits with no clear path, or no clear end date.

Access

Year-round road access for construction, heavy equipment deliveries and the people who run the site, in every season.

  • Roads that carry heavy loads all year
  • Distance to rail, ports or supply yards for big equipment
  • Safe access for staff in every season

Rules a site out: access that closes for part of the year with no fix.

Scoring

Every site, measured the same way

We score each candidate on all six criteria before we spend money on it. A strong site does not need to be perfect, but it cannot have a gap that ends the project.

Fuel supply carries the most weight. Without it, nothing else matters.

Process

From prospect to powered site

  1. Prospect

    We map energy, fiber and land data to find candidate sites, and talk to the people who own them.

    • Energy, pipeline and landfill data
    • Fiber routes and land records
    • Conversations with owners

  2. Diligence

    Gas volumes and contracts, land title, fiber routes, permits and access, checked on the ground.

    • Site visits
    • Gas and title review
    • Permit path mapped

  3. Design

    Generation, power, cooling and halls laid out around a tenant's requirements.

    • Layout and phases
    • Generation and redundancy
    • Cooling for the tenant's density

  4. Build and operate

    Construction, commissioning and day-to-day operation by our team.

    • Construction and testing
    • Tenant moves in
    • On-site operations for the lease

Site selection FAQ

Why not build where the grid is?

Grid connections for large new loads can take years, and the best-connected areas are crowded. Generating on site lets us build where firm energy already is. See the interconnection queue.

Which regions are you looking at?

Canada and the United States, wherever firm energy, fiber and land come together. We do not publish specific locations before agreements are in place.

How much land does a site need?

It depends on the tenant's power and layout. We look for room beyond the first phase so a site can grow without moving.

Does a site have to be near a city?

Not always. Workloads that serve users need low latency, while training can run far away. Staff also need to live within reach.

What usually rules a site out?

Short-lived or interruptible gas, no realistic fiber connection, permits with no clear path, or access that closes for part of the year.
